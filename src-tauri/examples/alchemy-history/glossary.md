A short glossary of the terms that recur across the sources in this notebook, from the names of substances to the stages of the work.

## Substances and principles

**Prima materia.** The first matter, the undifferentiated stuff from which all bodies were thought to be formed and to which the alchemist had to reduce the starting material before rebuilding it.

**Sulfur and mercury.** In Arabic and medieval theory, the two principles of which every metal is composed: sulfur for combustibility and earthiness, mercury for fusibility and metallic shine. Not the ordinary substances, though those were their nearest examples. Paracelsus added **salt**, the principle of solidity and the residue left after burning, making the **tria prima**.

**Elixir.** From the Arabic al-iksir, the agent of transmutation. In Western usage a synonym for the philosopher's stone; in Chinese and Indian usage, a preparation taken to prolong life.

**Philosopher's stone.** The perfected substance, usually described as a heavy red powder, that could convert base metals to gold by projection and cure disease when taken internally. The **white stone**, an earlier stage, converted to silver.

**Aqua fortis, aqua regia, oil of vitriol, spirit of salt.** Alchemical names for nitric acid, the nitric-hydrochloric mixture that dissolves gold, sulfuric acid, and hydrochloric acid.

**Sal ammoniac.** Ammonium chloride, a reagent introduced by Arabic alchemists and valued because it sublimes and attacks metals.

**Vitriol.** Metal sulfates, chiefly of iron (green vitriol) and copper (blue vitriol). Heated, they yield sulfuric acid.

**Azoth.** A Paracelsian name for the universal solvent or for philosophical mercury. The word packs the first and last letters of the Latin, Greek, and Hebrew alphabets.

**Quintessence.** The fifth essence, incorruptible like the heavens, extracted from wine or other bodies by repeated distillation; the medical alchemy of the fourteenth century was built on it.

## Apparatus

**Alembic.** Originally the head of a still where vapor condenses; later the whole still. From the Arabic al-anbiq and the Greek ambix.

**Cucurbit.** The gourd-shaped flask at the base of a still.

**Retort.** A flask with a long downward-bent neck that combines boiler and condenser.

**Aludel.** A stack of pear-shaped pots for sublimation.

**Athanor.** A self-feeding charcoal furnace that holds a steady heat for days, used for the long digestion of the sealed vessel.

**Bain-marie.** The water bath, named for Maria the Jewess.

**Kerotakis.** A closed vessel in which metal leaves were exposed to rising vapors; the ancestor of reflux apparatus.

**Pelican.** A circulating vessel whose side arms return the distillate to the body.

**Philosophical egg.** The sealed glass flask in which the Great Work was carried out.

**Lute.** The clay paste used to seal joints. A **hermetic seal** was a glass neck fused shut.

**Cupel.** A cup of bone ash used in assaying: lead and base metals oxidize into it, leaving a bead of gold and silver.

## Operations

**Calcination.** Heating a solid until it becomes a powder or ash.

**Sublimation.** Vaporizing a solid and collecting it again as a solid.

**Distillation.** Vaporizing a liquid and condensing it; **cohobation** is repeated distillation of the same liquid.

**Putrefaction.** Slow decomposition under gentle heat, the blackening stage.

**Fixation.** Making a volatile substance stable in fire.

**Multiplication.** Increasing the quantity or the power of the finished stone.

**Projection.** Casting the stone onto molten metal to transmute it.

**Spagyric.** Paracelsus's term for his method of separating a body into its principles and recombining them purified.

## Stages and symbols

**Nigredo, albedo, citrinitas, rubedo.** The blackening, whitening, yellowing, and reddening that mark the progress of the Great Work in the sealed vessel. The **peacock's tail** is the display of colors between black and white.

**Magnum opus.** The Great Work: the whole process of making the stone.

**Chymical wedding, coniunctio.** The union of opposites, pictured as the marriage of a red king and a white queen, that produces the stone. The **rebis** is the two-headed figure that results.

**Ouroboros.** The serpent swallowing its tail, a symbol of the unity and circularity of the work, already present in Greek texts.

**Decknamen.** Cover names: the coded vocabulary of dragons, lions, and eagles under which alchemists described substances and operations.

**Chrysopoeia.** Gold-making, the branch of chymistry concerned with transmutation.

**Chymistry.** The historians' spelling for the seventeenth-century discipline before alchemy and chemistry separated.

**Iatrochemistry.** Chemical medicine in the Paracelsian tradition.

**Waidan and neidan.** Chinese external alchemy (the furnace) and internal alchemy (the body).

**Rasashastra.** The Indian science of mercury.

**Adept.** One who was believed to have completed the work and possessed the stone.
