Alibaba Group Holding Limited (BABA; HKG: 9988), headquartered in Hangzhou, is China's e-commerce and cloud pioneer: Taobao and Tmall marketplaces at home, AliExpress and Trendyol internationally, Cainiao logistics, and Alibaba Cloud, China's largest cloud provider. In fiscal 2025, which ended in March 2025, Alibaba reported revenue of roughly RMB 996 billion (about $138 billion) and net income of roughly RMB 130 billion (about $18 billion).

The period marked a rehabilitation. After the regulatory crackdown era and a shelved restructuring plan, Alibaba refocused on two priorities: e-commerce, where it defended share against PDD and Douyin with user-first investments and instant-commerce delivery wars that pressured margins; and AI plus cloud, where the story brightened dramatically. The Qwen open-model family became one of the world's most-used open-weight LLM lines, cloud revenue reaccelerated to double-digit growth on AI demand, and the company committed hundreds of billions of yuan to AI infrastructure over multiple years — a buildout that re-rated the stock sharply in 2025. Other themes: partnership to power AI features in Chinese iPhones; quick-commerce subsidy battles with Meituan and JD; continued international commerce growth; large buybacks shrinking the share count; and the return of founder-era energy under CEO Eddie Wu with Jack Ma reappearing publicly. Geopolitics — chip access and U.S. listing questions — remains the wildcard.
