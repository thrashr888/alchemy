Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD), headquartered in Santa Clara, designs CPUs and GPUs across data center (EPYC processors, Instinct AI accelerators), client PCs (Ryzen), gaming (Radeon, console chips), and embedded (Xilinx). In fiscal 2024, AMD reported revenue of about $26 billion and GAAP net income of roughly $1.6 billion, with the data center segment roughly doubling to become half the company on EPYC server share gains and the first meaningful year of Instinct MI300 AI accelerator sales — about $5 billion.

Strategic themes: the pursuit of NVIDIA in AI compute, with the MI300/MI325/MI350 line iterating annually toward the rack-scale MI400 "Helios" systems, and the ROCm software stack as the persistent gap to CUDA; the landmark 2025 OpenAI partnership — a multi-year, multi-gigawatt deployment agreement that included warrants for OpenAI to take a stake of up to roughly 10 percent of AMD, instantly validating the roadmap and re-rating the stock; continued server CPU share gains against Intel with double-digit percentage points captured; export controls that wrote off China-bound accelerator inventory in 2025; the ZT Systems acquisition for rack-scale integration; and client strength on AI PC cycles. AMD's bet is that the AI compute market is large enough for a strong second source — and customers agree.
