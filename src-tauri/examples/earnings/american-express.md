American Express Company (AXP), headquartered in New York, runs a closed-loop payments network: unlike Visa and Mastercard it issues its own cards, extends the credit, and operates the network, earning a mix of discount fees from merchants, annual card fees, and interest income. Its franchise skews premium — affluent consumers and small businesses — with the Platinum and Gold cards as flagships. In fiscal 2024, American Express reported revenue net of interest expense of about $66 billion and net income of roughly $10 billion, both records.

Strategic themes: the deliberate shift toward fee-based economics, with net card fee revenue compounding in the high teens as members accept ever-higher annual fees in exchange for lush credits and lounges — the 2025 Platinum refresh pushed the U.S. consumer fee to $895; generational capture, with Millennials and Gen Z driving the majority of new premium accounts, answering the perennial "aging customer base" critique; credit quality consistently better than banking peers, reflecting the affluent book, even as loans grow faster than the industry; premium-card competition escalating with JPMorgan's Sapphire Reserve and Citi's entrants, forcing an arms race in perks; international merchant acceptance expansion; steady technology and marketing spending near the top of the industry; and dependable capital returns through buybacks and a growing dividend, with Berkshire Hathaway as the anchor shareholder.
