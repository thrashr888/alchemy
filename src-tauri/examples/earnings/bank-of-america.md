Bank of America Corporation (BAC), headquartered in Charlotte, is the second-largest U.S. bank, spanning consumer banking, global wealth and investment management (Merrill), global banking, and global markets. In fiscal 2024, Bank of America reported revenue of about $102 billion and net income of roughly $27 billion. Net interest income — the bank's largest revenue source — troughed mid-2024 as deposit costs peaked, then began rebuilding as fixed-rate assets repriced higher.

Strategic themes: the swing factor of the securities portfolio, where low-yielding bonds bought during the pandemic suppressed earnings but roll off steadily into higher yields; consumer credit that normalized rather than deteriorated, with charge-offs stabilizing through the period; strong trading and a recovery in investment banking fees; sustained technology spending, including the Erica virtual assistant and a broad internal AI rollout across developers and bankers; organic growth adding consumer checking households and wealth advisors while the branch network consolidates; and capital returns through rising dividends and buybacks under evolving Basel capital proposals. Long-time CEO Brian Moynihan continued to emphasize "responsible growth." Berkshire Hathaway, historically the largest shareholder, trimmed its stake substantially during 2024–2025, an overhang the market absorbed. Results track the U.S. rate cycle more closely than any megabank peer.
