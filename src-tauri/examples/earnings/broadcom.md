Broadcom Inc. (AVGO), based in Palo Alto, is a semiconductor and infrastructure software company assembled through serial acquisition. Its chip business spans networking silicon (Tomahawk and Jericho switch chips), custom AI accelerators (XPUs) designed with hyperscale customers, broadband, wireless components for smartphones, and storage; its software arm includes VMware, acquired for about $69 billion in late 2023, plus CA and Symantec.

In fiscal 2024, ended November 2024, Broadcom reported revenue of about $52 billion, up sharply on the VMware consolidation, with GAAP net income of roughly $6 billion — depressed by acquisition amortization — and much larger adjusted earnings. AI revenue (custom accelerators plus networking) exceeded $12 billion for the year and became the company's growth engine, with fiscal 2025 AI revenue guided far higher on demand from a small set of hyperscale customers designing their own training chips.

Strategic themes: Broadcom's position as the leading alternative to NVIDIA for AI compute via custom silicon co-design; the aggressive VMware integration — subscription-only licensing and steep price increases that drew customer complaints but expanded software margins dramatically; multi-gigawatt XPU orders announced with additional customers, including a landmark OpenAI partnership in 2025; and a shareholder-return model of rising dividends funded by acquisition cash flows.
