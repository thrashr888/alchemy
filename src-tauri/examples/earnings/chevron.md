Chevron Corporation (CVX), headquartered in Houston after relocating from California in 2024, is the second-largest U.S. oil major, with upstream operations concentrated in the Permian Basin, Kazakhstan, the Gulf of Mexico, and Australia LNG, plus downstream refining and chemicals. In fiscal 2024, Chevron reported revenue of nearly $200 billion and net income of roughly $18 billion, down year over year on softer refining margins and lower prices.

The defining event was the acquisition of Hess Corporation — announced at about $53 billion in 2023 and closed in mid-2025 after Chevron prevailed in arbitration against ExxonMobil over rights to Hess's 30 percent stake in Guyana's Stabroek block, the industry's premier growth asset. The deal adds Guyana's low-cost barrels and Bakken production to a portfolio already growing through the expanded Tengiz project in Kazakhstan, which reached its target rates in 2025 after years of cost overruns. Other themes: record Permian output approaching a million barrels per day with capital spending held flat; a structural cost-reduction program targeting billions in savings; steady dividend growth backed by one of the sector's strongest balance sheets, plus large buybacks throttled to commodity prices; new ventures in lithium and gas-fired power for data centers; and lingering exposure to Venezuela policy swings.
