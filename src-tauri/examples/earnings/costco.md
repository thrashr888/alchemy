Costco Wholesale Corporation (COST), headquartered in Issaquah, Washington, operates a membership warehouse club model: roughly 900 warehouses worldwide selling a deliberately limited assortment at razor-thin markups, with profit driven largely by membership fees. In fiscal 2024, ended September 2024, Costco reported revenue of about $254 billion and net income of roughly $7.4 billion, with membership fee income around $4.8 billion and renewal rates above 90 percent — the highest customer loyalty in retail.

Strategic themes: the first membership fee increase in seven years took effect in September 2024 ($65 Gold Star, $130 Executive in the U.S. and Canada), a high-margin tailwind flowing through fiscal 2025; comparable sales growth that consistently outpaced the industry, with strength in both groceries and a surprising run in gold bars and luxury items; steady international expansion, where warehouses in Asia produce some of the chain's best volumes; e-commerce and Costco Logistics growth for big-and-bulky delivery; a retail media business in its infancy relative to peers, seen as a future profit lever; and the Kirkland Signature private label, whose quality reputation lets Costco discipline national-brand pricing. The perennial investor complaint is valuation: the stock trades at a premium multiple far above other retailers, justified by the annuity-like membership economics.
