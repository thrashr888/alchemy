Eli Lilly and Company (LLY), headquartered in Indianapolis, is the most valuable pharmaceutical company in the world, a position built on its incretin (GLP-1) franchise: tirzepatide, sold as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity. In fiscal 2024, Lilly reported revenue of about $45 billion, up more than 30 percent, and net income of roughly $10.5 billion; growth continued at a similar clip into 2025 as manufacturing capacity caught up with demand.

Strategic themes: the obesity market, plausibly one of the largest in pharmaceutical history, where Lilly and Novo Nordisk form a de facto duopoly and Lilly's tirzepatide showed superior weight-loss efficacy in head-to-head data; orforglipron, Lilly's oral GLP-1 pill, which posted positive late-stage results in 2025 and could vastly expand access since it needs no injection or cold chain; tens of billions of dollars committed to new manufacturing sites in Indiana, North Carolina, and abroad to break the supply bottleneck; pricing and access fights, including direct-to-consumer channels (LillyDirect), compounding pharmacies selling copies during shortage periods, and political pressure on U.S. drug prices; and a deep pipeline beyond incretins — Alzheimer's therapy donanemab (Kisunla) approved in 2024, plus oncology and immunology programs. Beyond incretins, Verzenio in oncology remains a multi-billion-dollar growth driver.
