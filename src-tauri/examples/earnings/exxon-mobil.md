Exxon Mobil Corporation (XOM), headquartered in Spring, Texas, is the largest Western oil and gas major, spanning upstream production, refining, chemicals, and a growing low-carbon solutions unit. In fiscal 2024, ExxonMobil reported revenue of about $340 billion and net income of roughly $34 billion — below the record 2022 windfall but strong by historical standards, supported by production growth rather than price.

Strategic themes: the $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Pioneer Natural Resources, closed in mid-2024, which made Exxon the dominant producer in the Permian Basin and lifted total output toward 4.5 million barrels of oil equivalent per day; the extraordinary Guyana development, among the most profitable oil projects in the world, where production ramped past 600,000 barrels per day; a structural cost-cutting program that has removed more than $10 billion of annual expenses since 2019; disciplined capital returns — a dividend raised for more than four decades running plus a large buyback program; and measured low-carbon bets in carbon capture, hydrogen, and lithium that management frames as molecule businesses adjacent to core skills. Exxon also litigated and lobbied prominently on climate disclosure rules while arguing oil demand will persist for decades. Results remain leveraged to commodity prices, which drifted lower through 2025.
