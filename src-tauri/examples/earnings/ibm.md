International Business Machines Corporation (IBM), headquartered in Armonk, New York, is the century-old enterprise technology company reshaped around hybrid cloud and AI: software (Red Hat, watsonx, automation, data), consulting, and infrastructure (z-series mainframes, Power servers). In fiscal 2024, IBM reported revenue of about $63 billion and net income of roughly $6 billion, with free cash flow above $12 billion — the metric management leads with.

Strategic themes: software as the growth engine, led by Red Hat OpenShift and an acquisition streak that added HashiCorp (about $6.4 billion, closed 2025) for infrastructure automation alongside Apptio and dozens of smaller deals; the watsonx platform and a "book of business" for generative AI — consulting signings plus software — that management reported growing into the billions, positioning IBM as the enterprise-grade, governance-first AI partner; consulting softness as clients paused discretionary projects, an ongoing drag; the z17 mainframe cycle launched in 2025 with on-chip AI acceleration, reliably lifting infrastructure revenue; quantum computing as the long-horizon differentiator, with a roadmap toward error-corrected systems that IBM markets more credibly than most rivals; and a dividend maintained for generations, now paired with renewed if modest revenue growth. The turnaround thesis — slow, steady, cash-generative relevance — largely held through the period.
