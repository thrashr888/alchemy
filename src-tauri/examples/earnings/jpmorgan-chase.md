JPMorgan Chase & Co. (JPM), headquartered in New York, is the largest U.S. bank by assets and the most profitable bank in the world. Its lines of business span consumer banking (Chase), credit cards, the corporate and investment bank, commercial banking, and asset and wealth management. In fiscal 2024, JPMorgan reported managed revenue of roughly $180 billion and record net income of about $58 billion — the largest annual profit in the history of American banking — helped by elevated net interest income, a rebound in investment banking fees, and strong markets revenue.

Strategic themes: the durability of net interest income as the Federal Reserve began cutting rates in late 2024; the bank's "fortress balance sheet" posture, with management under Jamie Dimon repeatedly warning about inflation, geopolitics, and fiscal deficits even while results kept breaking records; heavy technology spending — well over $15 billion a year — including a firm-wide AI rollout that put an internal LLM assistant in front of most employees; the First Republic acquisition of 2023 continuing to add earnings; branch expansion while rivals shrink; and the long-running question of succession for Dimon, the industry's longest-tenured chief executive. JPMorgan also returns enormous capital through dividends and buybacks while holding excess capital against proposed Basel rules.
