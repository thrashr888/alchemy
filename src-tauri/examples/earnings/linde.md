Linde plc (LIN), headquartered in Woking, UK, with major operations run from the U.S. and Germany, is the world's largest industrial gases company, formed by the 2018 Linde–Praxair merger. It supplies oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, argon, and specialty gases to steel, chemicals, electronics, healthcare, and food customers through on-site plants, pipelines, and packaged gases, plus the Linde Engineering plant-building business. In fiscal 2024, Linde reported revenue of about $33 billion and net income of roughly $6.6 billion, with operating margins that have expanded for years under a famously disciplined pricing and productivity culture.

Strategic themes: the resilience of the model — long-term take-or-pay contracts, dense distribution networks, and pricing power that held earnings growth steady even as industrial production in Europe stagnated; a project backlog in the billions weighted toward clean energy, including blue and green hydrogen plants and carbon-capture infrastructure, where Linde invests only against contracted returns; electronics gases riding semiconductor fab construction, including supply agreements for new leading-edge fabs; steady bolt-on acquisitions of packaged-gas distributors; and shareholder returns through more than three decades of consecutive dividend increases plus substantial buybacks. Linde is often cited as the quality-compounder archetype among industrials: unglamorous molecules, extraordinary consistency.
