Mastercard Incorporated (MA), based in Purchase, New York, operates the world's second-largest card payments network. Like Visa, it is a toll-taker on electronic payments rather than a lender, earning assessments on volume and fees on switched transactions, plus a fast-growing services business spanning cyber and fraud tools, data analytics, and consulting. In fiscal 2024, Mastercard reported net revenue of about $28 billion and net income of roughly $13 billion, with gross dollar volume in the range of $9–10 trillion.

Strategic themes: cross-border travel and spending as the highest-margin growth driver; the services segment growing faster than the payments core and marketed as a differentiator against Visa; the long secular runway of cash-to-card conversion in emerging markets, which Mastercard courts through fintech partnerships and national digital-payment schemes; tokenization and agentic-commerce initiatives, including standards work aimed at letting AI agents transact safely; interchange regulation and merchant litigation as perennial legal overhangs, alongside scrutiny of network fees in the U.S., UK, and EU; and acquisitions concentrated in security and identity (most notably Recorded Future, acquired in 2024 for about $2.65 billion). Mastercard consistently posts mid-teens earnings growth and returns most free cash flow through buybacks and a rising dividend.
