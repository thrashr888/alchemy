Merck & Co., Inc. (MRK), headquartered in Rahway, New Jersey, is a research-driven pharmaceutical company whose modern identity is dominated by Keytruda, the cancer immunotherapy that became the world's best-selling drug. In fiscal 2024, Merck reported revenue of about $64 billion and net income of roughly $17 billion; Keytruda alone contributed around $29 billion, roughly the largest single-product franchise in the industry, spanning dozens of tumor types and adjuvant settings.

Strategic themes: the 2028 U.S. patent cliff for Keytruda, the central question hanging over the company, answered with a subcutaneous formulation launched in 2025 to extend the franchise, an enormous business-development spree (the roughly $10 billion Verona acquisition for COPD drug Ohtuvayre in 2025, ADC partnerships with Daiichi Sankyo and Kelun, cardiometabolic deals), and a late-stage pipeline management values at tens of billions in potential revenue; the collapse of Gardasil sales in China — once a major growth engine — due to inventory gluts and weak demand, forcing shipment pauses and guidance cuts; Winrevair, the pulmonary arterial hypertension drug from the Acceleron acquisition, ramping as a blockbuster; animal health as a steady diversifier; and Medicare price negotiation touching the diabetes franchise. Merck maintains a reliable dividend and heavy R&D spending near a quarter of sales.
