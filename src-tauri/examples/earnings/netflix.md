Netflix, Inc. (NFLX), headquartered in Los Gatos, is the world's largest subscription streaming service, with over 300 million paid memberships by the end of 2024. In fiscal 2024, Netflix reported revenue of about $39 billion and net income of roughly $8.7 billion, with operating margins expanding past 25 percent — a decisive answer to the 2022 doubts about streaming economics.

Strategic themes: the paid-sharing crackdown, which converted password borrowers into tens of millions of new memberships through 2024; the advertising tier, which reached meaningful scale and became the growth engine after Netflix stopped reporting quarterly subscriber counts in 2025, shifting the narrative to revenue and margin; an expanding live and events strategy — NFL Christmas games, boxing spectacles, WWE weekly programming — aimed at advertisers and cultural moments; continued investment of roughly $17–18 billion a year in content, increasingly localized across Korea, India, Japan, and Europe; a growing games portfolio; and price increases across tiers absorbed with little churn, evidence of pricing power. Competitive context: rivals consolidated and cut content spending while Netflix extended its engagement lead — about two hours of viewing per member per day. The stock's debate shifted to how much further margins and advertising can compound from an already dominant position.
