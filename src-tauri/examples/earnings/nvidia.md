NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA), based in Santa Clara, designs the GPUs and systems that power modern AI. Its data center segment — accelerators such as the Hopper and Blackwell generations, plus networking from the Mellanox acquisition — now dwarfs the gaming business the company was built on. In fiscal 2025, which ended in January 2025, NVIDIA reported revenue of about $130 billion, more than doubling the prior year, with net income of roughly $73 billion — extraordinary margins for a hardware company, reflecting scarce supply and pricing power.

Strategic themes: the ramp of the Blackwell platform and the transition to rack-scale systems (NVL72) sold to hyperscalers and AI labs; the CUDA software moat that keeps developers on NVIDIA silicon; heavy customer concentration among a handful of cloud providers, and those customers' parallel efforts to build in-house accelerators; U.S. export controls that largely shut NVIDIA out of the Chinese data center market, with successive China-specific chips caught in shifting rules; and supply constraints at TSMC, its manufacturing partner, for advanced packaging. NVIDIA became one of the first companies to reach a multi-trillion-dollar market capitalization on the AI buildout, making its quarterly earnings a de facto referendum on the entire AI trade.
