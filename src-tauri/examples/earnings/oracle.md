Oracle Corporation (ORCL), headquartered in Austin, sells database software, enterprise applications (Fusion, NetSuite), and — the story of this period — Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI). In fiscal 2025, which ended in May 2025, Oracle reported revenue of about $57 billion and net income of roughly $12 billion, with cloud services the fastest-growing line.

The defining development was AI infrastructure demand: Oracle positioned OCI as a cost-effective place to train and serve large models, winning marquee capacity deals with OpenAI (as part of the Stargate data center venture with SoftBank), Meta, xAI, and others. Remaining performance obligations — contracted future revenue — exploded to hundreds of billions of dollars during 2025, at one point adding more than $300 billion from a single OpenAI agreement, and the stock had its best day in decades on the disclosure. The counterweights: capital expenditure guidance ballooned toward $35 billion and beyond, free cash flow turned negative, and analysts questioned concentration risk on a handful of AI customers whose own finances depend on continued fundraising. Other themes: multicloud partnerships that place Oracle databases inside AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud; steady growth in Fusion and NetSuite applications; the TikTok U.S. hosting relationship; and a leadership transition naming two co-CEOs in late 2025 while Larry Ellison remained chairman and CTO.
