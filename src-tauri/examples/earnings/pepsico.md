PepsiCo, Inc. (PEP), headquartered in Purchase, New York, is a global convenient-foods and beverage company — uniquely balanced between snacks (Frito-Lay: Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos), beverages (Pepsi, Gatorade, Mountain Dew), and foods (Quaker), with roughly 60 percent of profits historically tied to food rather than drinks. In fiscal 2024, PepsiCo reported revenue of about $92 billion and net income of roughly $9.6 billion. Organic growth decelerated sharply from the inflation-era highs as North American snack volumes turned negative — consumers pushed back on years of price increases and traded toward value.

Strategic themes: reviving Frito-Lay North America, the profit engine that stumbled on affordability perceptions, with smaller pack sizes, sharper price points, and stepped-up marketing; portfolio modernization toward "permissible" snacking — the Siete Foods and Poppi acquisitions, sugar reduction, and removing artificial ingredients amid U.S. political attention on processed food; the Quaker recalls of 2024 that dented the foods segment; international business as the consistent grower, from India to Mexico; GLP-1 weight-loss drugs as a slow-burning demand question for salty snacks; an activist stake (Elliott Management) disclosed in 2025 pressing for turnaround urgency; and a dividend-aristocrat record of more than fifty consecutive annual increases. Execution in North America remains the swing factor.
