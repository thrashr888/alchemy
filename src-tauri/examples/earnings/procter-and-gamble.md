The Procter & Gamble Company (PG), headquartered in Cincinnati, is the world's largest consumer staples company, with brands including Tide, Pampers, Gillette, Crest, Olay, Downy, and Charmin across fabric care, baby care, grooming, oral care, and beauty. In fiscal 2024, which ended in June 2024, P&G reported net sales of about $84 billion and net income of roughly $15 billion; fiscal 2025 sales were roughly flat at a similar level as volume growth stayed sluggish.

Strategic themes: the shift from price-led to volume-led growth after two years of aggressive inflation-era pricing — a transition that proved slow, with consumers trading down in some categories and volumes soft in China, P&G's second-largest market, where the SK-II beauty brand struggled; steady productivity programs funding brand investment ("superiority" strategy across product, packaging, communication, go-to-market, and value); a restructuring announced in 2025 trimming several thousand non-manufacturing roles and exiting some categories and markets; tariff exposure on imported materials managed through reformulation and pricing; and an unbroken record of shareholder returns — nearly seven decades of consecutive dividend increases, plus buybacks, returning well over $15 billion a year. P&G's results are read as a gauge of consumer resilience and pricing power in developed markets.
