Roche Holding AG (SWX: ROG), headquartered in Basel, Switzerland, is one of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies and the global leader in both oncology and in-vitro diagnostics, with U.S. biotech pioneer Genentech as its research crown jewel. In fiscal 2024, Roche reported group sales of about CHF 60.5 billion and net income of roughly CHF 9 billion, with the pharmaceuticals division around three-quarters of sales and diagnostics the remainder.

Strategic themes: growth carried by a newer generation of medicines — multiple sclerosis therapy Ocrevus, hemophilia drug Hemlibra, eye-disease injection Vabysmo, and breast cancer franchise Phesgo — which fully absorbed the long biosimilar erosion of the old Avastin/Herceptin/Rituxan trio; a rebuilt pipeline push into cardiovascular, metabolism, and obesity, headlined by the Carmot acquisition's incretin candidates and a large 2025 partnership with Zealand Pharma for amylin analog petrelintide, positioning Roche as a credible third entrant behind Lilly and Novo; heavy U.S. manufacturing and R&D investment commitments announced in 2025 amid tariff and drug-pricing pressure; diagnostics normalization after the COVID testing boom, with growth led by immunodiagnostics and pathology; Alzheimer's diagnostics and a renewed disease-modifying pipeline; and the unusual governance of founding-family control plus a large cross-holding history with Novartis. Roche pays one of Europe's most dependable dividends, raised for decades.
