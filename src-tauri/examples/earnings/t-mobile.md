T-Mobile US, Inc. (TMUS), headquartered in Bellevue, Washington, is the second-largest U.S. wireless carrier by subscribers and the industry's growth leader since its 2020 merger with Sprint. Majority-influenced by Deutsche Telekom, it serves well over 100 million customers. In fiscal 2024, T-Mobile reported revenue of about $81 billion and net income of roughly $11 billion, alongside industry-best postpaid phone customer additions.

Strategic themes: the durable 5G network advantage built on Sprint's mid-band spectrum, which T-Mobile converted into both subscriber growth and premium network rankings while rivals spent heavily to catch up; 5G fixed wireless home internet, where T-Mobile leads the industry and keeps raising subscriber targets; expansion into fiber through joint ventures and the Lumos and Metronet deals, plus acquisitions of UScellular's wireless operations (closed 2025) and Mint Mobile to consolidate spectrum and subscribers; the shift from growth story to capital-return story, with tens of billions authorized in buybacks and a growing dividend; satellite-to-phone service with SpaceX's Starlink (T-Satellite), commercialized in 2025 as a coverage differentiator; leadership transition announced for late 2025, with COO Srini Gopalan succeeding long-time CEO Mike Sievert; and intensifying competition from cable operators' mobile offerings at the value end of the market.
