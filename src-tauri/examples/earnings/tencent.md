Tencent Holdings Limited (HKG: 0700), headquartered in Shenzhen, is China's most valuable company: the operator of WeChat/Weixin (over 1.3 billion users), the world's largest video game business (Honor of Kings, PUBG Mobile, plus stakes in Riot, Supercell, and Epic), a leading fintech operation (WeChat Pay), cloud services, and a vast investment portfolio. In fiscal 2024, Tencent reported revenue of roughly RMB 660 billion (about $90 billion) and net income of roughly RMB 194 billion (about $27 billion), with profit growing much faster than revenue as the mix shifted to high-margin lines.

Strategic themes: a gaming recovery at home and abroad after the 2021–2023 regulatory freeze, with evergreen titles re-accelerating and new licenses flowing; the "trinity" of high-quality revenue — video accounts (short video inside WeChat), mini-games, and AI-lifted advertising — driving margin expansion; the Hunyuan model family and aggressive integration of AI (including its Yuanbao assistant) across advertising, gaming, and cloud, with capex stepped up though far below U.S. hyperscaler levels; U.S.–China friction, including a brief 2025 designation on a U.S. military-related list that Tencent contested, and chip-supply constraints shaping its AI buildout; steady divestment gains from its portfolio; and among the largest buyback programs in Hong Kong, which supported the stock's re-rating.
