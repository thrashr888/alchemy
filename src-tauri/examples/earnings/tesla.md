Tesla, Inc. (TSLA), based in Austin, makes electric vehicles (Model 3, Y, S, X, Cybertruck), energy storage systems (Megapack, Powerwall), and solar products, and is increasingly pitched by its CEO as an AI and robotics company. In fiscal 2024, Tesla reported revenue of about $98 billion and GAAP net income of roughly $7 billion; vehicle deliveries of around 1.8 million were slightly below the prior year — the first annual decline — while the energy storage business roughly doubled and became a meaningful profit contributor.

Strategic themes: intense EV price competition, especially from Chinese manufacturers led by BYD, which compressed automotive margins well below their 2022 peak; the pivot of the investment narrative toward autonomy — a robotaxi service launched in Austin in 2025 with safety monitors aboard, supervised Full Self-Driving sold as a software subscription, and the Optimus humanoid robot program; the aging vehicle lineup and a lower-cost model long promised but repeatedly delayed; the loss of U.S. EV tax credits under 2025 legislation; and governance drama around Elon Musk's compensation, his political activities' effect on the brand in the U.S. and Europe, and a shareholder-approved trillion-dollar pay package tied to extraordinary milestones. Tesla's valuation continues to price in far more than its car business earns.
