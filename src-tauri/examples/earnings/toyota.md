Toyota Motor Corporation (TM; TSE: 7203), headquartered in Toyota City, Japan, is the world's largest automaker by volume, selling roughly 10–11 million vehicles a year across the Toyota and Lexus brands, with major operations in Japan, North America, and Asia. In fiscal 2025, which ended in March 2025, Toyota reported revenue of roughly ¥48 trillion (about $320 billion) and net income of roughly ¥4.8 trillion (about $32 billion) — near-record profitability helped by a weak yen and rich product mix, though below the prior year's extraordinary peak.

Strategic themes: vindication of the hybrid-first strategy, as global demand swung toward hybrids — where Toyota holds commanding share and strong margins — while battery-EV adoption slowed in Western markets; a deliberate "multi-pathway" roadmap spanning hybrids, plug-ins, battery EVs (with next-generation models and battery plants in the U.S. and Japan), and hydrogen; U.S. tariffs imposed in 2025, which Toyota absorbed at a cost measured in hundreds of billions of yen while accelerating localization; certification-testing scandals at group companies (Daihatsu, Toyota Industries) that dented the group's quality halo; a corporate-governance push including the planned buyout of Toyota Industries; and steady investment in Woven City, software-defined vehicles, and solid-state battery research. Toyota's scale and cash generation remain the industry benchmark.
