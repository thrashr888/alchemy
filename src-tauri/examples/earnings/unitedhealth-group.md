UnitedHealth Group Incorporated (UNH), based in Minnetonka, Minnesota, is the largest U.S. health care company, combining UnitedHealthcare (the biggest health insurer) with Optum (pharmacy benefits, care delivery clinics, and health data services). In fiscal 2024, UnitedHealth reported revenue of about $400 billion and net income of roughly $14 billion — profits dented by the massive Change Healthcare ransomware attack, whose remediation and business disruption costs ran into the billions, and by the costs of exiting Brazil.

The 2024–2025 period was the most turbulent in the company's history: the murder of UnitedHealthcare's chief executive in December 2024 put the insurer's claim-denial practices under a national spotlight; medical costs in Medicare Advantage ran persistently hotter than priced, forcing a rare guidance withdrawal in 2025; the long-serving CEO departed abruptly and former chief Stephen Hemsley returned to steady the company; and reports of Justice Department investigations into Medicare billing practices pressured the stock, which lost roughly half its value from its peak before partially recovering — a decline notable enough that Berkshire Hathaway disclosed a new stake. Structural themes remain: Optum's vertical integration of insurance, pharmacy, and physician practices; regulatory scrutiny of pharmacy benefit managers; and repricing the Medicare book back to margin targets through 2026.
