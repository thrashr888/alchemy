Visa Inc. (V), headquartered in San Francisco, operates the world's largest payments network, connecting cardholders, merchants, and banks across more than 200 markets. Visa does not lend; it earns fees on payment volume, transactions processed, and a growing set of value-added services. In fiscal 2024, ended September 2024, Visa reported net revenue of about $36 billion and net income of roughly $20 billion — margins among the best of any large company — on payment volume in the neighborhood of $15 trillion.

Strategic themes: steady growth in cross-border volume, the most lucrative transaction type, as international travel normalized; expansion beyond cards into "new flows" (account-to-account, B2B, Visa Direct push payments) and value-added services (risk, fraud, advisory), which now grow faster than the core; stablecoins and real-time payment systems as both a threat narrative and an integration opportunity — Visa piloted stablecoin settlement and positions itself as the network of networks; regulatory and legal pressure, including the U.S. Department of Justice antitrust suit filed in 2024 over debit network practices and long-running interchange litigation with merchants; and consistent capital return through buybacks and dividends. Visa's transaction data gives it one of the cleanest real-time reads on global consumer spending, which stayed resilient through the period.
