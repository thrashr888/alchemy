Walmart Inc. (WMT), based in Bentonville, Arkansas, is the world's largest retailer by revenue, operating Walmart U.S., Walmart International, and Sam's Club. In fiscal 2025, which ended in January 2025, Walmart reported revenue of about $681 billion and net income of roughly $19 billion. Comparable sales in the U.S. grew steadily through the period, driven by grocery share gains — including from higher-income households trading in — and a rapidly scaling e-commerce operation that approached profitability on the strength of store-fulfilled delivery.

Strategic themes: the transformation of Walmart's economics by higher-margin side businesses — advertising (Walmart Connect, plus the acquired Vizio ad platform), the Walmart+ membership program, and third-party marketplace and fulfillment services — which now contribute a disproportionate share of profit growth; automation of distribution and fulfillment centers to cut delivery costs; competition with Amazon on speed, with a growing share of orders delivered same-day; tariff exposure on imported goods and the balancing act of absorbing versus passing through costs, which management navigated publicly during 2025; and international strength in Mexico (Walmex), India (Flipkart and PhonePe), and China. Walmart's scale in groceries — roughly a quarter of U.S. grocery spending — makes its results a barometer for the American consumer.
